🔬 Tinbergen's Questions

A comprehensive framework for understanding behavior in biological and social systems

🎯 Function (Adaptation)
What problem does this behavior solve? How does it help the organism or system survive and reproduce?
Example for Evolution of Ethnocentrism:

Ethnocentric cooperation may help groups compete more effectively against other groups, providing survival and reproductive advantages to group members. It can solve the problem of free-riding within groups by creating clear in-group/out-group boundaries.

⚙️ Mechanism (Causation)
How does the behavior work? What are the immediate causes and processes that produce it?
Example for Evolution of Ethnocentrism:

Ethnocentric behavior works through simple rules: cooperate with those who share your ethnic marker, defect with those who don't. In the model, this is implemented as conditional cooperation based on agent shape/color. The mechanism involves recognizing ethnic markers and applying different cooperation strategies accordingly.

🌱 Development (Ontogeny)
How does the behavior develop over an individual's lifetime? What experiences or learning shape it?
Example for Evolution of Ethnocentrism:

In humans, ethnocentric tendencies develop through socialization, cultural learning, and experience with in-group and out-group members. Children learn ethnic categories and associated attitudes from family, peers, and media. In the model, strategies are inherited at "birth" but can mutate, representing cultural transmission with variation.

🧬 Evolution (Phylogeny)
How did this behavior evolve over evolutionary time? What selective pressures shaped it?
Example for Evolution of Ethnocentrism:

Ethnocentrism likely evolved through multilevel selection: groups with strong in-group cooperation may have outcompeted other groups, even if within-group selection favored selfishness. Kin selection and frequency-dependent selection also play roles. The model shows how local interaction and offspring placement can favor ethnocentric strategies.
