# BeeSmart Hive Finding

Shows the swarm intelligence of honeybees during their hive-finding process. A swarm of tens of
thousands of honeybees can accurately pick the best new hive site available among dozens of
potential choices through self-organizing behavior.

The mechanism in this model is based on *Honeybee Democracy* (Seeley, 2010) with some
modifications and simplifications. One simplification is that this model only shows scout
bees — a 3–5% population of the whole swarm that is actively involved in the decision-making
process. Other bees are left out because they simply follow the scouts to the new hive once a
decision is made.

This model is also the first of a series of models in a computational modeling-based scientific
inquiry curricular unit "BeeSmart," designed to help high school and university students learn
complex-systems principles as crosscutting concepts in science learning. (The source model page
lists further models in that unit — a HubNet participatory-simulation variant, "Multiple Check
World," "Debate World" — as "coming soon"; none of those are included in this repo.)

## How it works

At each SETUP, 100 scout bees are placed at the center of the view, and a number of potential
hive sites (set by the `hive-number` slider) are randomly placed around the swarm. On GO, initial
scouts (`initial-percentage` of the population) fly out to explore for up to
`initial-explore-time` ticks. A scout that discovers a site inspects it, then flies home to
advertise it through a waggle dance — the better the site's quality, the longer and more
"enthusiastic" the dance, and the more likely idle bees are to follow it and inspect the site
themselves. Interest in a site (and therefore the length of the dance advertising it) declines a
little on each repeat visit, so lower-quality sites are gradually abandoned in favor of
higher-quality ones. Once enough bees are gathered at one site to exceed the `quorum` slider's
threshold, those bees fly home and start to "pipe," a signal that spreads through the swarm; once
every bee is piping, the whole swarm commits to that site and the model stops. No single bee, and
no external controller, ever decides which site wins — the decision is an emergent property of
many local waggle-dance interactions.
