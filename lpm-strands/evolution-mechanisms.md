# Multi-Model Comparison: Evolution Mechanisms

> **Extended Learning Material** - Cross-model exploration of evolutionary mechanisms

## Overview

This guide provides a structured progression through multiple NetLogo models to build deep understanding of how natural selection works in different contexts, from simple predator-prey dynamics to complex social and spatial environments.

## Learning Progression

### Stage 1: Basic Natural Selection
**Model:** [Bug Evolution](../models/bug-evolution/)

**Focus:** The core mechanism of natural selection in a simple setting

**Key Concepts:**
- Trait variation in populations
- Differential survival and reproduction
- Inheritance of traits
- Gradual change over generations

**Activities:**
1. Run the model with different predation pressures
2. Observe how speed changes over time
3. Record observations about survival and reproduction
4. Discuss: Why do some traits become more common?

**Thinking Tools:**
- Tinbergen's Questions: Function, mechanism, development, evolution of speed
- The Noticing Tool: What patterns emerge in trait frequencies?

**Time:** 45 minutes

---

### Stage 2: Population Structure and Founder Effects
**Model:** [Island World](../models/island-world/)

**Focus:** How spatial structure and migration affect evolution

**Key Concepts:**
- Local adaptation to different environments
- Founder effects and genetic drift
- Migration and gene flow
- Balance between local selection and migration

**Activities:**
1. Compare islands with different resource levels
2. Experiment with different migration rates
3. Observe how populations diverge or converge
4. Discuss: Why do populations on different islands remain different?

**Thinking Tools:**
- Causal Mapping: Diagram how environment → selection → local adaptation
- Tinbergen's Questions: How does migration affect evolution?

**Time:** 60 minutes

---

### Stage 3: Evolution of Social Strategies
**Model:** [Evolution of Ethnocentrism](../models/evolution-ethnocentrism/)

**Focus:** How cooperation strategies evolve through natural selection

**Key Concepts:**
- Kin selection and positive assortment
- Multilevel selection (within-group vs. between-group)
- Frequency-dependent selection
- Ethnic markers and conditional cooperation

**Activities:**
1. Compare outcomes with different numbers of ethnicities
2. Experiment with local vs. global interaction
3. Use BehaviorSpace to test payoff matrix effects
4. Discuss: How can ethnocentrism evolve despite its costs?

**Thinking Tools:**
- Payoff Matrix: Manipulate A, B, C, D parameters
- Tinbergen's Questions: Function, mechanism, development, evolution of ethnocentrism
- Causal Mapping: Map how ethnic markers → interaction patterns → strategy success

**Assessment:** [Integrated Causal Reasoning Assessment](../assessments/evolution-ethnocentrism.json)

**Time:** 90 minutes

---

### Stage 4: Evolution with Resource Competition
**Model:** [Evolution and Competition for Forest Resources](../models/evolution-competition-forest-resources/)

**Focus:** How resource competition shapes evolution

**Key Concepts:**
- Competition for limited resources
- Carrying capacity
- Evolution of harvest strategies
- Population dynamics and evolution

**Activities:**
1. Experiment with different resource levels
2. Observe how population size affects evolution
3. Compare sustainable vs. unsustainable strategies
4. Discuss: How does resource scarcity affect evolution?

**Thinking Tools:**
- Causal Mapping: Diagram resource competition → selection pressure → strategy evolution
- Analogies: Compare to real-world resource competition

**Time:** 60 minutes

## Synthesis Activities

### Activity 1: Mechanism Comparison Matrix
Create a table comparing all four models on:
- Source of variation
- Selection pressure(s)
- Inheritance mechanism
- Time scale of change
- Role of environment
- Population structure effects

### Activity 2: Causal Chain Analysis
Map the causal chain for each model:
```
Environment → Selection Pressure → Differential Survival → Reproduction → Trait Frequency Change
```
How does each stage differ across models?

### Activity 3: Design Your Own
Design a new evolution model that combines elements from multiple models:
- Add spatial structure to Bug Evolution
- Add resource competition to Island World
- Add migration to Evolution of Ethnocentrism

### Activity 4: Real-World Application
Choose a real-world evolutionary phenomenon and:
1. Identify which model(s) best represent it
2. Use the model to generate insights
3. Propose research questions based on model understanding

**Examples:**
- Antibiotic resistance in bacteria
- Pesticide resistance in insects
- Climate change adaptation in species
- Evolution of human cooperation

## Competency Development

**OE-CONCEPT-bio-core-natural-selection: Natural Selection**
- Progression from simple to complex selection mechanisms
- Understanding variation, differential survival, inheritance, and time
- Recognizing context-dependence of selection

**OE-CONCEPT-bio-core-adaptation: Adaptation**
- How different environments favor different traits
- Trade-offs in adaptation
- Local vs. general adaptation

**OE-CONCEPT-oe-interdisciplinary-agency: Agency**
- Distinguishing individual agency from population-level evolution
- Understanding that evolution has no goals or purposes
- Avoiding anthropomorphism

## Common Misconceptions Addressed

1. **"Evolution has a goal"** - Explore how selection is context-dependent, not goal-directed
2. **"Individuals evolve"** - Learn that evolution happens to populations, not individuals
3. **"Faster/bigger is always better"** - Examine trade-offs and context-dependence
4. **"Evolution is just random"** - Understand the role of selection in shaping variation
5. **"Evolution always leads to improvement"** - Recognize that adaptation is environment-specific

## Teacher Notes

### Prerequisites
- Basic understanding of evolution (for Stages 2-4)
- Familiarity with NetLogo interface
- Ability to interpret graphs and data

### Differentiation
- **Grades 6-8:** Focus on Stages 1-2, qualitative observations
- **Grades 9-12:** Complete all stages, include quantitative analysis
- **Undergraduate:** Add original experiment design, literature connections

### Assessment Tips
- Use pre-assessment to identify misconceptions
- Encourage metacognition about reasoning patterns
- Focus on process, not just correct answers
- Use thinking tools to scaffold complex reasoning

### Extension Ideas
- Connect to current events (antibiotic resistance, climate change)
- Invite guest speakers (evolutionary biologists, medical researchers)
- Student research projects on real-world evolution
- Debate: "Is evolution predictable?"
