# Multi-Model Comparison: Cooperation Progression

> **Extended Learning Material** - Cross-model exploration of cooperation mechanisms

## Overview

This guide provides a structured progression through multiple NetLogo models to build deep understanding of cooperation, from simple two-agent dilemmas to complex multi-group dynamics with cultural evolution.

## Learning Progression

### Stage 1: Two-Agent Dilemma
**Model:** [Two Foresters](../models/two-foresters/)

**Focus:** Individual vs. collective interest in the simplest possible setting

**Key Concepts:**
- Cooperation vs. defection
- Social dilemma structure
- Short-term vs. long-term outcomes
- Trust and agreement-breaking

**Activities:**
1. Run the model with different harvest rates
2. Observe what happens when one forester breaks an agreement
3. Discuss: Why is cooperation difficult even when it benefits both?

**Thinking Tools:**
- Payoff Matrix: Map costs/benefits of different strategies
- Causal Mapping: Diagram how individual choices affect collective outcomes

**Time:** 45 minutes

---

### Stage 2: Group-Level Cooperation
**Model:** [Two Communities](../models/two-communities/)

**Focus:** How cooperation works when groups, not just individuals, are the units

**Key Concepts:**
- Group composition effects
- Within-group vs. between-group dynamics
- Critical mass of cooperators
- Group identity and norms

**Activities:**
1. Compare outcomes with different proportions of modest vs. greedy foresters
2. Experiment with different group compositions
3. Discuss: How does group size and composition affect cooperation?

**Thinking Tools:**
- Tinbergen's Questions: Analyze function and mechanism of group cooperation
- The Noticing Tool: What patterns emerge in group vs. individual outcomes?

**Time:** 60 minutes

---

### Stage 3: Ethnic Markers and Conditional Cooperation
**Model:** [Evolution of Ethnocentrism](../models/evolution-ethnocentrism/)

**Focus:** How ethnic markers shape cooperation strategies through evolution

**Key Concepts:**
- In-group favoritism
- Conditional cooperation (cooperate with same, defect with different)
- Kin selection and positive assortment
- Multilevel selection
- Evolution of social categories

**Activities:**
1. Compare outcomes with 1, 2, 4, and 6 ethnicities
2. Experiment with local vs. global interaction
3. Use BehaviorSpace to test payoff matrix effects
4. Discuss: Why might ethnocentrism evolve despite its costs?

**Thinking Tools:**
- Payoff Matrix: Manipulate A, B, C, D parameters
- Tinbergen's Questions: Function, mechanism, development, evolution of ethnocentrism
- Causal Mapping: Map how ethnic markers → interaction patterns → strategy success

**Assessment:** [Integrated Causal Reasoning Assessment](../assessments/evolution-ethnocentrism.json)

**Time:** 90 minutes

---

### Stage 4: Punishment and Monitoring
**Model:** [Evolution of Resource Use and Social Behavior](../models/evolution-resource-use-social-behavior/)

**Focus:** How punishment and monitoring mechanisms can sustain cooperation

**Key Concepts:**
- Second-order free-rider problem
- Costly punishment
- Monitoring and detection
- Institutional solutions to dilemmas

**Activities:**
1. Compare outcomes with and without punishment
2. Experiment with different punishment costs
3. Discuss: When is punishment effective? When does it fail?

**Thinking Tools:**
- Causal Mapping: Diagram punishment → deterrence → cooperation
- Analogies: Compare to real-world policing and justice systems

**Time:** 60 minutes

---

### Stage 5: Cultural Evolution of Cooperation
**Model:** [Evolution of Resource Use Through Behavior Imitation](../models/evolution-resource-use-behavior-imitation/)

**Focus:** How cooperation norms spread through cultural learning, not just genetic evolution

**Key Concepts:**
- Cultural transmission
- Imitation biases (conformist, success-based, etc.)
- Social norms and traditions
- Gene-culture coevolution

**Activities:**
1. Compare different imitation biases
2. Experiment with innovation rates
3. Discuss: How do cultural norms evolve differently from genetic traits?

**Thinking Tools:**
- Tinbergen's Questions: How does cultural transmission differ from genetic inheritance?
- Analogies: Compare to fashion trends, language change, technological adoption

**Time:** 75 minutes

## Synthesis Activities

### Activity 1: Comparison Matrix
Create a table comparing all five models on:
- Number of agents
- Types of strategies
- Mechanisms for cooperation
- Role of evolution
- Real-world applications

### Activity 2: Causal Chain Analysis
Map the causal chain from individual decisions to population-level outcomes across all models:
```
Individual decision → Interaction pattern → Payoff → Fitness → Strategy frequency → Population outcome
```
How does each stage differ across models?

### Activity 3: Design Your Own
Design a new cooperation model that combines elements from multiple models:
- Add ethnic markers to Two Communities
- Add cultural learning to Evolution of Ethnocentrism
- Add punishment to Two Foresters

### Activity 4: Real-World Application
Choose a real-world cooperation problem (climate change, fisheries, public health) and:
1. Identify which model(s) best represent it
2. Use the model to generate insights
3. Propose interventions based on model understanding

## Common Misconceptions Addressed

1. **"Cooperation is always good"** - Explore when cooperation can be harmful (e.g., in-group favoritism)
2. **"People cooperate because they're nice"** - Understand evolutionary and structural bases of cooperation
3. **"More cooperation is always better"** - Examine costs and trade-offs
4. **"Cooperation requires conscious intention"** - Learn about emergent cooperation from simple rules
5. **"Once cooperation evolves, it stays"** - Study conditions that can undermine cooperation

## Teacher Notes

### Prerequisites
- Basic understanding of evolution (for Stages 3-5)
- Familiarity with NetLogo interface
- Ability to interpret graphs and data

### Differentiation
- **Grades 6-8:** Focus on Stages 1-2, qualitative observations
- **Grades 9-12:** Complete all stages, include quantitative analysis
- **Undergraduate:** Add original experiment design, literature connections

### Assessment Tips
- Use pre-assessment to identify misconceptions
- Encourage metacognition about reasoning patterns
- Focus on process, not just correct answers
- Use thinking tools to scaffold complex reasoning

### Extension Ideas
- Connect to current events (international conflicts, climate agreements)
- Invite guest speakers (political scientists, evolutionary biologists)
- Student research projects on real-world cooperation problems
- Debate: "Is ethnocentrism inevitable?"
